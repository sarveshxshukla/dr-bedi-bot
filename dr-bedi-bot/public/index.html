Cancers Dr. Bedi treats

Every plan is built around the type of cancer, its stage, and what the biopsy and genetic tests show. Care covers early cancer through stage 4, and cancer that has come back.

Breast cancer
From a new breast lump to HER2-positive, triple-negative or hormone-sensitive cancer. Treatment for early-stage, stage 2 and 3, metastatic and recurrent breast cancer, in women and in men.
Lung cancer
Non-small cell (adenocarcinoma, squamous) and small cell lung cancer, including a nodule or mass found on a scan. Oral targeted therapy for EGFR mutations or ALK changes, immunotherapy and chemotherapy at every stage.
Bladder cancer
Chemotherapy before surgery to shrink the tumour, immunotherapy, and newer targeted treatment for advanced or metastatic bladder cancer in men and women.
Blood cancers
Leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma and other blood disorders, drawing on haemato-oncology training at The Royal Marsden, London, with guidance on newer options such as CAR T-cell therapy.
Prostate and testicular cancer

Ovarian and cervical cancer

Colon, stomach and pancreas

Skin cancer, melanoma and sarcoma

Treatment options under one roof

A medical oncologist treats cancer with medicines rather than an operation. When surgery or radiation is the better choice, Dr. Bedi plans it together with the surgical and radiation teams at Fortis.

Chemotherapy and day-care chemo

Most cycles are given in day care, so you go home the same day. Doses are adjusted to how your body responds.

Immunotherapy

Medicines such as pembrolizumab (Keytruda) help your own immune system find and attack cancer cells, alone or with chemotherapy.

Targeted therapy and precision oncology

Genomic testing of the tumour shows which mutation drives the cancer, so treatment can aim at it. Many of these are tablets, such as osimertinib for EGFR lung cancer.

Hormone therapy

For hormone-sensitive breast and prostate cancers, to slow growth and lower the chance of the cancer coming back.

CAR T-cell and cell therapies

Advice on whether newer T-cell treatments suit your blood cancer, and referral when they do.

Palliative and supportive care

Relief from pain, nausea and tiredness at every stage, so daily life stays as comfortable as possible.

A cancer specialist who takes the time to explain

A cancer diagnosis brings a hundred questions at once. Dr. Bedi's consultations start with your reports: he checks the biopsy, scans and blood tests, confirms the stage, and tells you plainly what the options are and what each one involves.

Complex cases are discussed in the Fortis tumour board, where medical, surgical and radiation oncologists agree on one plan together. That means you get a single, joined-up recommendation instead of different advice from different departments.

Families from Mohali, Chandigarh, Panchkula, Zirakpur and across Punjab come to him for first treatment and for second opinions. Consultations are in Hindi, Punjabi or English, whichever the family is most comfortable with.

Questions families often ask

Can't find yours? Write it in the form, or call the appointment line on 98779 95253.

What does a medical oncologist do?
A medical oncologist treats cancer with medicines: chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy and hormone therapy. They also coordinate the overall plan. Surgery and radiation are done by surgical and radiation oncologists, and at Fortis all three review complex cases together.
Can a tumour be treated without surgery?
Often, yes. Blood cancers and many advanced cancers are treated mainly with medicines. For other cancers, chemotherapy or immunotherapy is given before or after surgery, sometimes shrinking the tumour so the operation can be smaller. Whether surgery is needed depends on the type and stage, and Dr. Bedi will explain the options honestly.
Can stage 4 or metastatic cancer be treated?
Stage 4 cancer, and cancer that has come back after treatment, is usually treated to keep it under control for as long as possible while keeping daily life comfortable. Immunotherapy and targeted therapy have changed what is possible for many people with advanced lung, breast and bladder cancer. What is realistic depends on the cancer type, genetic test results and overall health.
What should I bring to my first appointment?
Biopsy reports, PET-CT, CT or MRI scans (with the CD if you have it), recent blood tests, and summaries from any hospital you have visited. A list of current medicines helps too. If tests haven't been done yet, come anyway, and Dr. Bedi will tell you which ones are needed.
Can I get a second opinion or consult online?
Yes. Many families come for a second opinion before starting chemotherapy, immunotherapy or surgery. Reports can be reviewed at the Mohali OPD, or you can begin with an online consultation. Choose "Other Cancers / Second Opinion" in the form and mention it in your message.
How much do the consultation and treatment cost?
The appointment team will tell you the current OPD consultation fee when you book. The cost of chemotherapy, immunotherapy or targeted therapy depends on the type and stage of cancer and the medicines used, so it is discussed once the treatment plan is clear.
Is there a "full body cancer check-up"?
No single test finds every cancer. If you have symptoms, such as a lump, a cough that won't settle, unexplained weight loss or bleeding, or a strong family history, see a cancer specialist promptly. Dr. Bedi can advise which screening tests are worth doing for your age and risk.
